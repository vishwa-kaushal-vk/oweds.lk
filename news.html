Sri Lanka, World Bank discussions on offshore wind development

October 6, 2026

World Bank representatives and senior Sri Lankan officials from the renewable energy sector held talks about the next stage of Sri Lanka’s Offshore Wind Phase III during a high-level meeting at the Energy Ministry on October 1.

The discussions focused on the findings of the prefeasibility study, the proposed next stage of development, and the way forward to advance the offshore wind programme.

A prefeasibility study on offshore wind development has identified an estimated 1,500 MW of potential offshore wind capacity in the Mannar and Puttalam areas of Sri Lanka. The findings highlight the significant opportunity available for the country to expand renewable energy generation through offshore wind resources.

Present at the meeting were Deputy British High Commissioner to Sri Lanka, Theresa O’Mahony, Energy Ministry Secretary G. M. R. D. Aponsu, World Bank Senior Infrastructure Finance Specialist, Prajakta AjitChitre, Sri Lanka Sustainable Energy Authority (SLSEA) Chairman Professor Wijendra J. Bandara and Economic Advisor of the British High Commission in Sri Lanka, Lakshila Wanigasinghe.

The meeting was described as highly successful, with constructive discussions between participating institutions and development partners. It was agreed that the next phase of the offshore wind development programme will be initiated shortly, marking another step towards strengthening Sri Lanka’s renewable energy capacity and advancing its transition to sustainable energy.
