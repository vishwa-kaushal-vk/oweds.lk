UK and EU launch joint solar energy initiative in Colombo

October 28, 2026

The British High Commission in Colombo and the Delegation of the European Union to Sri Lanka have launched a joint solar energy initiative aimed at reducing carbon emissions and promoting sustainable energy practices.

The initiative was officially launched today (28) in the presence of Colombo Mayor Vraie Cally Balthazaar, British High Commissioner Andrew Patrick, EU Ambassador-designate Sylvia Hartleif and Deputy British High Commissioner Theresa O’Mahony.

According to the British High Commission, the project involves approximately 240 kilowatts-peak (kWp) of installed solar capacity across its office premises, which is expected to generate 365,000 kilowatt-hours (kWh) of electricity annually.

The initiative is projected to reduce carbon dioxide emissions by approximately 193 tonnes each year.

The launch included a ceremonial switch-on of the solar system and a showcase of other environmentally sustainable initiatives undertaken by the British High Commission.

The solar project forms part of broader efforts to reduce the High Commission’s environmental footprint through energy-efficient cooling, lighting and building systems, along with solar water heating. These measures are expected to save approximately 79,200 kWh of electricity annually.

Other sustainability initiatives include 10,000 litres of rainwater storage capacity, a vehicle fleet comprising 25% plug-in hybrid vehicles, and waste segregation, recycling and composting measures accounting for approximately 1.13 tonnes of waste annually.

British High Commissioner Andrew Patrick said the initiative demonstrates the shared commitment of the UK and European Union towards a cleaner future through practical cooperation.

Meanwhile, EU Ambassador-designate Sylvia Hartleif said the project reflects the European Union’s broader support for Sri Lanka’s Green Recovery, including the promotion of renewable energy, climate resilience and access to affordable, sustainable energy.

Deputy British High Commissioner Theresa O’Mahony emphasised that sustainability extends beyond solar energy to responsible water use, waste management and transportation practices.

The British High Commission stated that the initiative highlights the importance of international partnerships in advancing renewable energy and environmentally sustainable operations in Sri Lanka.

Sri Lanka, World Bank discussions on offshore wind development

October 6, 2026

World Bank representatives and senior Sri Lankan officials from the renewable energy sector held talks about the next stage of Sri Lanka’s Offshore Wind Phase III during a high-level meeting at the Energy Ministry on October 1.

The discussions focused on the findings of the prefeasibility study, the proposed next stage of development, and the way forward to advance the offshore wind programme.

A prefeasibility study on offshore wind development has identified an estimated 1,500 MW of potential offshore wind capacity in the Mannar and Puttalam areas of Sri Lanka. The findings highlight the significant opportunity available for the country to expand renewable energy generation through offshore wind resources.

Present at the meeting were Deputy British High Commissioner to Sri Lanka, Theresa O’Mahony, Energy Ministry Secretary G. M. R. D. Aponsu, World Bank Senior Infrastructure Finance Specialist, Prajakta AjitChitre, Sri Lanka Sustainable Energy Authority (SLSEA) Chairman Professor Wijendra J. Bandara and Economic Advisor of the British High Commission in Sri Lanka, Lakshila Wanigasinghe.

The meeting was described as highly successful, with constructive discussions between participating institutions and development partners. It was agreed that the next phase of the offshore wind development programme will be initiated shortly, marking another step towards strengthening Sri Lanka’s renewable energy capacity and advancing its transition to sustainable energy.
